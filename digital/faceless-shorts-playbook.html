THE PLAYBOOK

Faceless Shorts

Post short videos every day without showing your face, using only free apps. Includes 60 hook templates, 5 formats, a 30-day calendar and a monetization map.

No income promises. This playbook gets you posting consistently, which is the part most people never get past.

How to use this playbook

  1. Today: pick your niche (Section 1). It takes 15 minutes.
  2. Tomorrow: set up the free workflow (Section 4) and make your first video.
  3. For 30 days: follow the calendar (Section 5). One video a day, about 30 minutes each.
  4. Every 7 days: do the 5-minute review (Section 6).
The only rule: post every day for 30 days before you judge anything. Most accounts look dead for the first 2–3 weeks. Short-form platforms test each video with a small audience first, and you need volume before one breaks through.

What you need

  • A phone
  • CapCut (free) for editing and auto-captions
  • Canva (free) for text screens and thumbnails
  • Free stock B-roll: Pexels, Pixabay, or CapCut's built-in library
  • Optional: the free voices in CapCut's text-to-speech, if you don't want to record your own

2. The 5 faceless formats

Format 1: List or countdown

"3 things…" or "5 apps…". One item per 2–3 seconds, big text on screen, B-roll behind it. Best for: any niche. The easiest to make.

Format 2: Screen-record tutorial

Record your phone or computer screen doing the thing. Zoom in on taps and add captions. Best for: tech, apps, finance tools.

Format 3: Text story over B-roll

A short story told in on-screen text (3–6 lines) over a satisfying or relevant clip. Best for: motivation, life lessons, "storytime".

Format 4: Voiceover explainer

You (or a text-to-speech voice) explain one idea in 20–40 seconds over B-roll and captions. Best for: facts, history, finance, education.

Format 5: Hands-only demo

Film your hands doing something: cooking, unboxing, a before/after, a DIY. No face needed. Best for: cooking, products, crafts, beauty.

Structure for every format:
0–2 s: the hook (text on screen and spoken).
2–20 s: the value, delivered fast. Cut every pause.
Last 2 s: a loop back to the start, or a call to action ("follow for part 2", "save this").

4. The free 30-minute workflow

StepWhat to doTime
1. ScriptWrite the hook plus 3–5 short lines. Read it out loud and cut any word you don't need.5 min
2. FootageFilm your hands or screen, or grab 3–5 free clips from Pexels or CapCut's library.8 min
3. Voice (optional)Record a voiceover in CapCut, or use its text-to-speech.3 min
4. Edit in CapCutNew project, 9:16. Drop in the clips and trim each to 1.5–3 s. Text → Auto captions. Put the hook text in big type at the top third.10 min
5. Export1080p, 30 fps. Upload natively to each platform, with no watermarks from other apps.2 min
6. PostCaption = hook + 3–5 relevant hashtags. Post at the same time every day.2 min

Safe zones

Keep important text out of the bottom ~20% and the right edge. That's where captions, buttons and usernames cover the video on TikTok, Reels and Shorts.

Batch it

Make 5–7 videos in one sitting on the weekend and schedule them. One session a week is how this stays manageable.

6. The 5-minute weekly review

  1. List this week's videos and their views after 48 hours.
  2. Circle the top 2. What do they share: the hook, format, topic or length?
  3. Next week, make 3 more versions of the winner. Change only the hook or the first 2 seconds.
  4. Drop any format that came in last 2 weeks in a row.
  5. Check your average watch time. If people leave in the first 3 seconds, the hook is the problem. If they leave in the middle, the video is too long.

The order that usually works

  1. Post daily until one format clearly wins.
  2. Add an affiliate link or a low-price product that matches that format.
  3. Turn on platform payouts when you qualify.
  4. Build an email list (a free checklist works) so you own your audience.
